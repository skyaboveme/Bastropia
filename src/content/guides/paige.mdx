Paige is a real town in the way people use the word, and it is not a city in the way Texas law uses the word. That single difference explains most of the practical questions: there is no city council to attend, no city water department to call, and no city trash calendar with your street on it. The county, the school district, and the utility that actually serves the address are the offices that can help.

## What Paige is

The Handbook of Texas, published by the Texas State Historical Association, places Paige on U.S. Highway 290 in northeast Bastrop County, about 44 miles east of Austin. State Highway 21, the Old San Antonio Road, borders the north side. The handbook says the town was established in 1872 at a railroad water tower on the new Houston and Texas Central Railway, and that it was named for Norman Paige, a civil engineer who laid out the route. A post office opened in 1874. In 1876 the station was moved three miles east, to the site the handbook treats as the present location.

The same entry says the handbook’s place type is a town, that it is not incorporated, and that it has a post office. The historical narrative describes a German farming and railroad shipping town, with cotton gins in the 1880s, a bank by 1914, and a population the handbook gives as 275 in both 1990 and 2000. This guide does not treat that figure as today’s headcount. The handbook’s narrative was last revised in 1995, and a population widget on the same web page does not match itself closely enough to quote. If you need a current Census number, use the Census, not this page.

The handbook also records that a volunteer fire department was established in 1982 and that the Paige Community Center was incorporated that year. That is a historical statement about those organizations being formed. It is not a current phone number or a promise that either one answers today. For a fire that is happening, call 911.

The [Paige town page](/paige) on this site is the weekly layer: the forecast and whatever listings name Paige. It is short on purpose. There is no city channel and no city council video to embed.

## Schools

Bastrop ISD’s about page says the district serves more than 13,000 students in Bastrop, Cedar Creek, Red Rock, Rockne, and Paige, across 433 square miles. The district office is 906 Farm Street in Bastrop, (512) 772-7100. The district publishes attendance-zone maps. The boundaries page reviewed for this guide included elementary, middle, and high school maps for 2026–2027. Colorado River Collegiate Academy and Genesis High School are described there as options for high school students across the district, with admission to the collegiate academy by application.

Use the map. “Paige” on a mailing address is a strong hint, and it is not a legal assignment. Rural district lines do not follow the old town plat. The district’s own boundaries page says the school a student attends is determined by where the student lives. If a house sits near McDade or toward Smithville, open those districts’ sites too before you register. This guide will not claim a three-district split for every Paige address, because no boundary map reviewed here drew that sentence. The safe instruction is the one the district prints: look up the address.

Closings, when Bastrop ISD posts one on its homepage, appear on the [school-status page](/schools) with the other three districts.

## County offices, because there is no city hall

Commissioners Court is the local lawmaking body for an unincorporated town. It meets in the Commissioners Courtroom on the second floor of the courthouse annex at 804 Pecan Street in Bastrop. The dates for 2026 and 2027 are on the court page, and the regular sessions on that list start at 9:00 a.m. The [government guide](/guides/bastrop-county-government) explains the public participation form and why the court page, as reviewed, does not offer a standing livestream. The [new-resident page](/new-here) links the precinct map. A road in Paige is a precinct question. It is not a question for Elgin or Smithville city hall.

The county’s main phone, printed on the county site, is (512) 581-7100. County Judge Gregory Klaus’s office is (512) 332-7201 at the courthouse. Hours on the judge’s page are 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Thursday and 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Friday, closed 12 to 1.

Property tax is collected by the Bastrop County Tax Assessor-Collector, Ellen Owens’s office. The locations page lists four counters: 211 Jackson Street in Bastrop, (512) 581-7161; 5785 FM 535 in Cedar Creek, (512) 581-4270; 1125 Dildy Drive in Elgin, (512) 581-7146; and 1624 NE Loop 230 in Smithville, (512) 581-7114. There is no Paige counter. Smithville’s substation is open Tuesdays and Fridays only, starting October 5, until further notice. The [moving guide](/guides/moving-to-bastrop-county) has the Comptroller’s January 31 due date, the February 1 delinquency date, and the April 30 homestead deadline. The homestead application goes to the Bastrop Central Appraisal District, not to the tax office.

U.S. 290 is the highway the handbook uses to locate the town. Closures and construction alerts for that road, when the state has filed them, are on the [roads page](/roads). The header of this site also shows a US 290 line taken from those alerts.

## Utilities, trash, and fire

Bluebonnet Electric Cooperative tells new members to check the service-area map before they start service. Where Bluebonnet is the provider, member services is 800-842-7708 and the outage line is 800-949-4414. Member services, on the co-op’s stop-service page, answers 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday. Aqua Water Supply Corporation’s contact page is 415 Old Austin Highway in Bastrop, 512-303-3943, including for an emergency leak. The lobby hours are 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed 12 to 1. This guide does not say every Paige address is on both systems. The map and the phone call are the check. Neither provider is a city department, because Paige does not have one.

Trash is the same idea. Bastrop, Elgin, and Smithville publish city routes. The [new-resident page](/new-here) links those calendars. Paige is not those cities, so those routes are not your pickup day. Neighbors outside the cities arrange hauling themselves. Ask the county or a hauler. Do not assume a city calendar covers FM roads east of Elgin.

The burn ban is countywide, and it applies in Paige. The county posts the order on its outdoor-burning page. This site repeats it at the [burn-ban page](/burn-ban). When this guide was updated, the county page said no ban was in effect. That sentence will be wrong on a dry week. Re-read the order. The Texas rule still limits what can be burned, and when, even with no ban. The [fire-safety guide](/guides/burn-bans-and-fire-safety) walks through the TCEQ conditions, including the ban on trash, treated wood, and rubber, and the rule that a burn under those requirements waits until an hour after sunrise and ends an hour before sunset. There is no Paige ordinance that creates a local exception. The Office of Emergency Management is at 1501 Business Park Drive in Bastrop, (512) 581-4022, and it asks residents to register with Warn Central Texas.

For a week of weather and whatever is listed in town, use the [Paige page](/paige). For a park day, Bastrop and Buescher are a drive west. They are written up in the [things-to-do guide](/guides/things-to-do-in-bastrop-county), with the fees those park pages actually print.
