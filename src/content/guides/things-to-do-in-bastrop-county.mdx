The county’s easy days out are mostly public land and a few downtown streets. The state parks, the LCRA lake parks, and the river trail publish their own hours and fees. This guide uses those pages, plus the city and Visit Bastrop pages for downtown and the annual events. It does not name restaurants or quote a private outfitter’s prices.

## Bastrop and Buescher state parks

Bastrop State Park is in the Lost Pines. Texas Parks & Wildlife says people have visited for the pines for more than 70 years, and that the park is about 32 miles east of Austin. The things-to-do list on the park page includes camping, picnics, swimming, bikes, hiking, fishing, geocaching, and nature programs. The page says there are seven miles of trail. An all-terrain wheelchair is available; the park says to call (512) 321-2101 to reserve it.

The address is 100 Park Road 1A, Bastrop, TX 78602. The park is open daily from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. The entrance fee listed on the park page is $5 a day for an adult and free for a child 12 and under. The interpretive center is listed Friday through Sunday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and the page says those hours may vary. TPWD says the park often fills and that a reservation is the way to guarantee entrance for camping or a day visit. Reservations are online or at (512) 389-8900. The park page says the busy season is spring, summer, and fall.

The park map adds the campfire rule neighbors ask about. If a burn ban is not in effect, campfires are permitted only in the fire rings at the sites. Ground fires are not. Gathering firewood is prohibited. Quiet hours on that map run from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Read the [burn-ban page](/burn-ban) the day you go. A county order overrides a pleasant forecast.

Buescher State Park is the next park east, with a Smithville address: 100 Park Road 1E, Smithville, TX 78957, phone (512) 237-2241. TPWD describes a 30-acre lake and nearly six miles of trail, including the Winding Woodlands Trail and the Pine Gulch Trail, plus a former state champion cedar elm called the Big Tree. You can hike, bike, fish, geocache, and camp. The page says no fishing license is needed in the park lake and that the park loans fishing gear. Cabins, screened shelters, and campsites with electricity or water are on the lodging list. The Civilian Conservation Corps built the lake and dam, a trail, and the group facilities in the 1930s.

Buescher is open daily from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. The listed day fee matches Bastrop: $5 for an adult, free for a child 12 and under, with a $3 adult group day-use rate also listed. TPWD again says the park often reaches capacity.

Both park pages describe Park Road 1C the same way: a hilly 12-mile road between Bastrop and Buescher through recovering and forested parts of the Lost Pines. You can bike it or drive it.

## Lake Bastrop

Lake Bastrop is an LCRA park lake, not a state park. LCRA operates Lake Bastrop North Shore Park at 603 FM 1441, Bastrop, TX 78602, (512) 578-4816, and Lake Bastrop South Shore Park at 375 South Shore Road, Bastrop, TX 78602, (512) 498-1922.

Both park pages say the grounds are open daily from sunrise to sunset, with a gift shop from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Entrance is listed as $5 for adults, free for children 12 and younger, and $2 for seniors 65 and older, military, and disabled visitors. An LCRA Parks Pass covers entry. North Shore lists a boat ramp, trails, a fishing pier, a swimming area, picnic tables, and rentals. South Shore lists those kinds of amenities plus mini golf and a wildlife viewing blind. Both pages list RV sites and cabins, with rates that start at different numbers and can change. Use the park page for the current rate rather than a number copied here.

Swimming at the LCRA parks is posted as at your own risk. The North Shore map says swimming is not allowed within 50 feet of a boat ramp and that there are no lifeguards.

## The Colorado River

The river through Bastrop is a TPWD paddling trail, the El Camino Real Paddling Trail. TPWD says it is a wide, slow-moving trail of about six miles and that the float takes about 1.5 to 4 hours depending on flow and how often you stop. The put-in is Fisherman’s Park, at the corner of Farm Street and Willow Street in downtown Bastrop. The take-out is Lost Pines Recreational Trails. TPWD notes that floods can close the take-out, so check conditions after rain.

The trail page says the lower Colorado is free-flowing below Longhorn Dam, but that flow through Bastrop is still affected by the Highland Lakes and by rainfall. It points paddlers to LCRA river information and to the USGS gauge at Bastrop, station 08159200. That is the same gauge on the [county metrics page](/metrics) and in the strip at the top of this site. The trail has a few minor rapids and no man-made obstructions, and TPWD says to avoid snags and debris. Sandbars show up often enough to use as rest stops. The banks are private. TPWD says the river is navigable, which allows use of the streambed and, if you must, the bank to portage a hazard. Other use of a private bank without permission can be trespassing.

TPWD’s trail page lists Bastrop River Company at 601 Chestnut Street, (512) 988-1154, among the partners. Rentals and shuttle prices are on that business, not restated here.

The desk has two river stories worth reading beside a paddle plan. One is the [Old Iron Bridge reopening](/news/bastrop-old-iron-bridge-reopens-dec-3). The other is the [Colorado River streambank project](/news/bastrop-colorado-river-streambank-project-comments-nov-24). Both cite their own sources.

## Downtown streets

Visit Bastrop’s downtown page calls Main Street one of the historic centers of town and says downtown has more than 130 historic structures, with the Colorado River a short walk away. It tells visitors to start at the Bastrop County Museum and Visitor Center, in a 1930s fire and police station. The 2025 Visit Bastrop visitor guide lists that museum at 904 Main Street, (512) 313-0904. The same guide lists Visit Bastrop at 1408 B Chestnut Street, (512) 332-8990, and visitbastrop.com.

This guide does not pick restaurants or shops. The museum and the downtown page are the orientation. The [Bastrop town page](/bastrop) has this week’s listings. The calendar is carrying the [2026 Holiday Historic Homes Tour](/events/bastrop-holiday-historic-homes-tour-2026-2026-12-12), listed by Explore Bastrop County for December 12, 2026, at the museum on Main Street. Confirm the day’s hours on that listing’s source before you go.

Smithville’s downtown is a separate city. The city parks page lists Railroad Park and Main Street Park among the city parks, and it says all Smithville parks are closed from 11:30 p.m. to 6 a.m. Riverbend Park has camping by the Colorado River. The city’s camping page says sites include electricity, at 30 and 50 amp, and water, with a dump station and a restroom building with showers. Reservations go through the recreation center at 106 Royston Street, (512) 237-3282, extension 7. The rate table is on that city page. Read it when you book, because holiday dates on the same page use a different daily rate.

Elgin’s downtown is the setting for Hogeye, covered below, and for the city’s Main Street program. The [Elgin town page](/elgin) and the [Elgin guide](/guides/elgin) have the council, the school district, and the festival.

## Annual events

A few events are posted as traditions, not one-off concerts.

The City of Elgin says the Hogeye Festival is a downtown street festival on the fourth Saturday of October, put on by the Elgin Main Street Board. The city says Elgin has been a Texas Main Street City since 1990. The 2026 page calls it the 39th annual Hogeye and lists Thursday, October 22, through Saturday, October 24, with festival hours of 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. on Saturday and free admission. The same page describes a pork cook-off, live music, and a children’s area. The year’s schedule is the city page, not a memory of last October.

Visit Bastrop says the Lost Pines Christmas Parade is every second Saturday in December in historic downtown Bastrop, and that admission is free. The 2026 page lists Saturday, December 12, 2026, at 6 p.m. The [events calendar](/events) is where this desk files dated listings once an organizer posts them. A Christmas-tree story is on the news page if you are planning that errand: [Christmas tree farms near Bastrop and Elgin](/news/christmas-tree-farms-near-bastrop-elgin-2026).

If a poster and a city page disagree, use the city page or the organizer linked from the calendar. Park fees in this guide were copied from the park pages on the day the guide was updated. A seasonal rate can move. The link at the bottom is the current one.
