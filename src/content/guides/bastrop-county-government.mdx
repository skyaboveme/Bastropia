County government and city government are easy to mix up from the outside. The county judge and the commissioners handle county business, including county roads and the orders that come through Commissioners Court. Bastrop, Elgin, and Smithville each run their own city council, their own police department, and their own utility customers. Paige, Cedar Creek, and McDade do not have city councils. If you live there, the county is the local government, and a city meeting will not answer a county-road question.

## Commissioners Court

The county’s Commissioners Court page says meetings are held at the county seat, in the Commissioners Courtroom on the second floor of the annex at the Bastrop County Courthouse, 804 Pecan Street, Bastrop, TX 78602. The page publishes a list of dates for 2026 and 2027. The regular sessions on that list, including November 23 and December 14, 2026, and the dates running through September 2027, are set for 9:00 a.m. One date is marked as a change from the usual pattern: June 7, 2027, is on the first Monday of that month and still starts at 9:00 a.m. Special sessions show up on the same page. A November 16, 2026, special session was also listed at 9:00 a.m.

The court page links a public participation form and the rules of procedure and decorum. It also links a Texas Association of Counties explanation of the court’s duties. Read that form before you plan to speak. The page reviewed for this guide does not link a standing livestream. Attend in the courtroom, or read the agenda.

Agendas for individual meetings are posted in the county’s CivicWeb document center. A regular-session notice reviewed there, for Monday, November 23, 2026, says the court will meet at 9:00 a.m. in the Commissioners Courtroom, second floor, 804 Pecan Street, under Texas Government Code Chapter 551, the Open Meetings Act. The [desk preview of that November 23 agenda](/news/bastrop-county-commissioners-court-nov-23-2026-agenda-preview) is a news story, not the agenda itself. After a meeting, when minutes are posted, the [court-decisions page](/government/court-decisions) carries a short summary and a link.

The [new-resident page](/new-here) names the sitting commissioners and links the precinct map. Use the map for your road. This guide does not copy the roster, because a term changes and the county page is the list.

## County offices

The phone on the county site’s footer is (512) 581-7100, at 804 Pecan Street. County Judge Gregory Klaus’s office is at the courthouse, (512) 332-7201. The judge’s page lists hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Thursday and 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Friday, closed noon to 1.

The Tax Assessor-Collector is a county office, separate from the appraisal district. The locations page names Ellen Owens. The main counter is 211 Jackson Street, (512) 581-7161. Substations are in Cedar Creek, Elgin, and Smithville. Hours and the Smithville Tuesday-and-Friday schedule are in the [moving guide](/guides/moving-to-bastrop-county). The appraisal district, not the tax office, handles homestead exemptions.

The Office of Emergency Management is at 1501 Business Park Drive, (512) 581-4022. That is the office tied to the burn ban and to Warn Central Texas. The [fire-safety guide](/guides/burn-bans-and-fire-safety) is the longer note. Sheriff, county clerk, district clerk, and the justices of the peace are listed, with the county links, on the [phone-number page](/phone-numbers) and the [government page](/government). Call 911 for an emergency. Do not send an emergency to a court email.

## City of Bastrop

The City of Bastrop says it uses a council-manager government. The council sets policy by ordinance and resolution. The city manager carries it out. The council is the mayor and five members, all elected at large, so every city voter may vote for any of the six places.

Regular meetings are the second and fourth Tuesday of the month at 6:30 p.m. in the Council Chambers at City Hall, 1311 Chestnut Street. The city’s rules say a regular meeting ends no later than 10 p.m. and that the council can vote to change a day or a time. Notices are posted at City Hall and on the city website. The city says every council meeting is broadcast live on the City of Bastrop Facebook page, and that a recording is posted to the city’s YouTube page about 48 to 72 hours later. The city phone for meeting questions is (512) 332-8800. Agendas and minutes are on the city’s meetings site, bastrop-tx.municodemeetings.com.

The [Bastrop town page](/bastrop) is the weekly layer: weather, school dates, and listings. Council video is the city’s, not this desk’s.

## City of Elgin

Elgin’s council page says meetings are the first and third Tuesday at 6:30 p.m. at the Library Annex. It also says the time and date are subject to change, that extra meetings can be called with three business days’ notice, and that agendas are posted three business days before a meeting. Minutes follow after the council approves them.

The page lists a mayor at large and two members for each of four wards. As listed when this guide was updated, the mayor at large is Theresa Y. McShan, with a term ending May 2028. The full roster, including who is mayor pro tem, is on the council page and will change at an election. People who need help attending under the Americans with Disabilities Act are asked to contact the city secretary at 512-229-3222, with 48 hours’ notice when they can. The city’s main number on the same site is 512-281-5724, at 310 N. Main Street.

The council page says past meetings and city videos are available from that page. This guide does not claim a live stream beyond what that sentence says. The [Elgin guide](/guides/elgin) covers the school district and Hogeye. A recent desk note on the city election is [Elgin voters and mixed-drink sales](/news/elgin-voters-approve-citywide-mixed-drink-sales).

## City of Smithville

Smithville’s council page says the city uses a council-manager government. The council adopts ordinances and resolutions. The city manager runs operations. The council is the mayor and five members, elected at large. As listed when this guide was updated, the mayor is Sharon Foerster. The staff directory lists Trey Job as city manager. The page asks council questions to go to City Secretary Jennifer Lynch at (512) 237-3282, extension 2106. City offices are at 317 Main Street, 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. Monday through Friday, phone (512) 237-3282.

The council page itself does not print a standing weekday and time in the overview. Agendas are in the city’s Agenda Center, and the city calendar lists meetings as they are set. A notice for November 9, 2026, set that meeting for 6:00 p.m. at City Hall, 317 Main Street. Other 2026 notices in the Agenda Center are a mix of regular packets and special called meetings, so the safe habit is to open the current notice. Do not treat one Monday or one Tuesday as the permanent rule.

The [Smithville guide](/guides/smithville) covers parks, Riverbend camping, and the school district. The [Smithville town page](/smithville) has this week’s listings.

## Which meeting you want

If the question is a county road, a burn ban, or a precinct budget, it belongs at Commissioners Court or with the commissioner for that precinct. If the question is a city water bill, a city ordinance, or a downtown festival, it belongs at that city council. Showing up at the wrong dais wastes the evening. The addresses above are the ones printed on the official pages. The [events calendar](/events) sometimes lists a posted hearing when an organizer or a government page has put it on a public calendar. The agenda on the government site is still the document that controls the meeting.
