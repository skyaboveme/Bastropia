Roof repairs at the historic Bastrop County Courthouse in downtown Bastrop are moving again after a bee infestation held up the work, Community Impact reported Nov. 18.

**The delay**

An Oct. 13 report to Bastrop County Commissioners Court pushed the deadline to finish the roof work from **Sept. 5 to Dec. 4**, according to Community Impact. The report pointed to three causes: the bees, scheduling conflicts and weather. Community Impact said county crews are now back at work.

The roof job is its own project, separate from the larger restoration of the building.

**The bigger restoration**

Bastrop County is also working on a **$23.4 million** historic preservation project to bring the courthouse back to its original look, Community Impact reported. According to county officials quoted in that report:

- The restoration process started in **2022**.
- It could take **six to 10 years** in all, which would put completion somewhere between **2028 and 2032**.
- Plans include reconstructing the original clock tower and other historic architectural details, updating the building's systems and making it more accessible.

**What it means for you**

The courthouse is still one of downtown Bastrop's landmarks, and residents who walk or drive Pecan and Main streets may see work crews and equipment around the building through early December. Community Impact did not report any office closures tied to the roof work. If you have business at a county office, check the office's page on bastropcounty.gov or call ahead before you go.

Progress reports on the courthouse projects come to Commissioners Court from time to time. The court's next regular session is Monday, Nov. 23, at 9 a.m. at 804 Pecan St. Agendas are posted on the county's CivicWeb portal at https://bastropco.civicweb.net/filepro/documents/122048/ and on the County Clerk's agendas page.

Bastropia will share future updates on the restoration timeline as the county posts them.
