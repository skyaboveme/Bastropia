If you live or work on Texas 71 in Bastrop County, your address could change. The goal is to keep fire trucks and ambulances from heading to the wrong place.

**The problem**

Bastrop County, the city of Bastrop and the city of Smithville each number Texas 71 their own way, and the road switches between "East" and "West" in different spots. That means the same street number can show up in more than one place along the highway.

Local officials say that has slowed real emergencies. Bastrop City Council member John Kirkland told the council on Oct. 27 that during the 2023 Powder Keg Fire, crews were first sent to the 1000 block of East Texas 71 inside the city of Bastrop, when the fire was at the same block number in the county, the Austin American-Statesman reported. Smithville Councilman Brandon Dunham told his council on Oct. 26 that responders realized the mistake only when they saw the smoke, according to the Elgin Courier.

**The proposal**

A county advisory committee is working on one system for the whole highway. It includes the county, the cities of Bastrop and Smithville, and the county's three emergency services districts. Kirkland said the committee recommends:

- Dropping "East" and "West" so the road is simply State Highway 71
- Mile-based numbers, starting at about **10000 at the Travis County line** and ending near **16000 at the Fayette County line**
- Odd numbers on the north side of the highway and even numbers on the south

County staff estimated earlier this year that **1,697 addresses** could be affected, including 1,080 homes and 529 businesses, Community Impact reported.

**When**

The target start date is **Aug. 1, 2027**. It was chosen to avoid the holidays and elections. Kirkland said the U.S. Postal Service can update addresses in bulk. The committee plans to send out information and hold at least one public meeting. Agreements between the county and cities also need approval first.

Neither city council took action at its October meeting. Kirkland said the Bastrop City Council will decide whether the city joins. Some business owners have raised concerns about cost. Carlos Liriano of Lost Pines Toyota told the council that business cards alone would cost his dealership at least $2,000 to replace.

**What you can do now**

Nothing changes yet. Watch for county mailers and public meeting notices, and plan to tell the committee how a change would affect your home or business.
