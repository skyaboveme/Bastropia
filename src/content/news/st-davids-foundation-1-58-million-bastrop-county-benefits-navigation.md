Three organizations that work in Bastrop County are getting foundation money to help residents enroll in — and hold onto — public benefits such as SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP and WIC.

Community Impact reported the awards Oct. 27, based on a St. David's Foundation announcement. The foundation is providing **$1.58 million** as part of its We All Benefit 2.0: Building Economic Stability program.

**Who received the grants**

- **Bastrop County Emergency Food Pantry:** The grant will pay for trained benefits navigators at county food pantries, to help eligible residents apply for SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP and Marketplace health coverage. The pantry says the work is meant to reduce barriers such as transportation, language, digital access and distance from services.
- **Career Tracks:** The Smithville-based organization will use its grant for a Bridge to Benefits Rural Collaborative in Bastrop County, focused on SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP and WIC. Community Impact reported the effort is aimed at rural households, older adults, people with disabilities and working families.
- **Texas Legal Services Center:** The grant supports a partnership with Community Health Centers of South Central Texas to help residents appeal denials, disenrollments and overpayment claims involving programs such as SSI, SSDI, Medicaid, CHIP, WIC and SNAP. Legal aid is to be offered through rural health centers.

The foundation did not publish the amount going to each organization in the Community Impact report.

**Why it matters**

Kim McPherson, a senior program officer at the foundation, told Community Impact that about one-third of residents struggle to pay their bills each month and that many may already qualify for help. Edward Burger, the foundation's CEO and president, said the grants are meant to make that safety net easier to use.

Over three years, foundation officials said they expect the broader program — which also funds organizations elsewhere in Central Texas — to connect more than **60,000** people to programs that can increase income and improve access to food and health care.

**How to get help**

If you're not sure whether you qualify, the groups say the first step is to ask.

- **Bastrop County Emergency Food Pantry**, 1201 Pine St., Bastrop. Phone: 512-303-0033. Website: https://bastropfoodpantry.org/. Check the pantry for current hours, especially around the holiday weekend.
- **Career Tracks**, 404-A Fawcett St., Smithville. Phone: 512-237-1108. Website: https://www.careertrackstx.org/.
- For help appealing a benefit decision, Community Impact said Texas Legal Services Center is working through **Community Health Centers of South Central Texas**.
