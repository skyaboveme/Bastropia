The City of Bastrop is asking anyone who may have touched or been close to either of two bats to come forward, after both tested positive for rabies.

According to the city, as reported by KXAN, [KVUE](https://www.kvue.com/article/life/animals/2-bats-test-positive-rabies-bastrop-texas/269-58fa72b1-c31e-4e62-b5ca-c4404915ede7) and the [Austin American-Statesman](https://www.statesman.com/news/local/article/bastrop-rabies-bat-exposure-22529791.php), the first bat was found Monday, Nov. 9, near the entrance of Bastrop's Walmart. The city said people could have been exposed from the time the store opened until noon that day. The Statesman reported that a second bat was found at storage units at 510 SH 71 W. The city shared both results on Friday, Nov. 13.

**If you think you had contact**

- Call Bastrop Animal Control at **512-332-8864** or **737-348-1956**.
- If you were bitten or scratched, or a bat touched your skin, call a doctor or the local health department right away. Don't wait to see if symptoms show up. The CDC advises washing any wound with soap and water and getting medical care promptly.
- The Texas Department of State Health Services runs a 24-hour line at **254-778-6744**.

**Why it matters**

Rabies spreads through an infected animal's saliva, usually through a bite or scratch. Once symptoms start, it's almost always fatal. Treatment given soon after exposure works very well. According to the CDC, bats are the animal most often reported with rabies in the U.S. The Statesman, citing Texas Health and Human Services, reported 234 bat rabies cases in Texas so far this year, many of them in the Austin–San Antonio corridor.

**Everyday precautions**

- Don't touch a bat, whether it looks alive, hurt, sick or dead. Teach kids to tell an adult instead.
- Keep pets away from bats, and keep dogs' and cats' rabies shots up to date.
- If you find a bat, keep people and pets back and call Animal Control.
- A bat that's out in daylight, on the ground, or flying poorly may be sick. KVUE notes those are common signs.

Bats are a normal part of life around Bastrop, and most don't carry rabies. These two cases are a good reminder to leave them alone and to call for help if someone has had contact.
