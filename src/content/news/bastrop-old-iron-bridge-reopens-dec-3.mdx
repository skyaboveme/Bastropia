After eight years behind fences, Bastrop's Old Iron Bridge is about to welcome walkers again.

The historic bridge over the Colorado River will reopen with a public celebration on **Thursday, Dec. 3**, after roughly **$11 million** in rehabilitation work, Community Impact reported Nov. 19.

**A century-old landmark**

The bridge was built in 1923 and is a Parker steel-truss design. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1990. Structural safety concerns closed it to pedestrians in 2018, and rehabilitation work began in 2025.

According to Community Impact, the work includes:

- A new concrete deck
- Repairs to the steel framework
- New railings
- Color-changing lights
- Stairs connecting the bridge to the June Hill Pape Riverwalk

**The Dec. 3 celebration**

- **4 p.m.:** Unveiling of the BTX Love Locks sculpture
- **4:30 p.m.:** Ribbon cutting
- **Around dusk:** The bridge's new color-changing lights are expected to switch on

**What about the love locks?**

Over the years, visitors clipped padlocks to the bridge as tokens of affection. Those locks were taken off before the rehabilitation, but they weren't thrown away. They will go on a new sculpture next to the bridge, and people can add new locks there. The idea is to keep the tradition going without putting extra weight and wear on the historic structure itself, so plan to leave your lock on the sculpture rather than the railings.

**Plan your visit**

The new stairs make it easier to combine a bridge walk with the June Hill Pape Riverwalk along the river downtown. If you go to the evening lighting, bring a jacket and allow time for parking downtown. Watch the City of Bastrop's channels for any final details on parking or schedule changes before Dec. 3.
