Live music has long been part of a night out in downtown Bastrop, and the city is now putting money behind it.

The Bastrop City Council this week approved a five-month pilot program that helps downtown businesses cover the cost of hiring musicians, KVUE reported Friday, Nov. 20.

**How the pilot works**

- **Who qualifies:** Businesses in Bastrop's Main Street district that host live music.
- **What the city pays:** A matching grant covering **30% of eligible musician fees**.
- **Limits:** Up to **$400 per performance**, with a cap of **$1,200 per business each month**.
- **Funding:** **$50,000** from the city's hotel occupancy tax revenue, the money collected from overnight hotel stays that Texas cities can spend on promoting tourism.
- **Length:** Five months.

KVUE did not report a start date or application details. Business owners interested in taking part should contact the City of Bastrop or its Main Street program for the application steps.

**Why the city is trying it**

Owners told KVUE that downtown foot traffic has slowed as Bastrop grows and new chain restaurants open elsewhere in town. Raymond Keyrouz, owner of 602 Brewing Company, told the station his business has booked music every Friday and Saturday since 2019, spent about $100,000 on live music last year, and had considered cutting it before the city stepped in.

Michaela Joyce, director of Discover Bastrop, the city's tourism office, told KVUE that music has long helped bring residents together and draw visitors to town.

**What happens next**

City officials told KVUE that if the pilot works, they could bring the results back to the council and ask for dedicated live music funding in future budgets.

For listeners, the takeaway is simple: if your favorite downtown spot has a band on the calendar this winter, the city may be covering part of the bill. You can find upcoming shows on the Bastrop County Calendar at https://bastropcountycalendar.com.
