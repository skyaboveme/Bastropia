## This week: Nov. 20, 1975 — the courthouse and jail join the National Register

Fifty-one years ago this week, on **Nov. 20, 1975**, the Bastrop County Courthouse and Jail Complex was added to the National Register of Historic Places. The listing is recorded by the National Park Service as reference number 75001947, and the Texas Historical Commission gives the same date.

The courthouse standing on the square today was built in **1883**, after the previous county courthouse burned that year. The old jail, a tan and red brick building on the same square, opened in **1892**. It was used as a jail into the 1970s and later converted to county office space. The two buildings were listed together.

The courthouse is still the county's landmark on the square, and it is in the middle of a long restoration. For a look at the current roof work, see Bastropia's Nov. 19 story, [Courthouse roof repairs resume after bee infestation](/news/bastrop-courthouse-roof-repairs-resume-bee-infestation/).

## This week: Nov. 23, 1759 — the Baron de Bastrop is born

**Nov. 23** is the birthday of the man whose name the county and the county seat still carry. According to the Texas State Historical Association, Felipe Enrique Neri, the self-styled Baron de Bastrop, was born **Philip Hendrik Nering Bögel** on Nov. 23, 1759, in Paramaribo, Dutch Guiana, now Suriname.

He later presented himself in Spanish Louisiana and then in Texas as a Dutch nobleman. Whatever the title, his practical role in Texas colonization is well documented. In 1820 he helped persuade the Spanish governor in San Antonio to approve Moses Austin's plan for an Anglo-American colony. He later worked with Stephen F. Austin and served in the legislature of Coahuila and Texas. He died in 1827 in Saltillo.

In 1837 the Congress of the Republic of Texas changed the name of Mina County, and the town of Mina, to Bastrop in his honor. A 1936 Texas Centennial monument to him stands on the courthouse grounds.

This Saturday, **Nov. 21**, the Bastrop County Historical Society Museum & Visitor Center is holding its [Baron's Birthday Bash](https://www.bastropcountyhistoricalsociety.com/event-details-registration/barons-birthday-bash) from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m. at 904 Main St. The party is two days before the historical birthday.

## This month in history: Nov. 18, 1891 — the Katy takes over a local railroad

Two days before this week, on **Nov. 18, 1891**, the Taylor, Bastrop and Houston Railway became part of the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railway Company of Texas, the line known as the Katy. The Handbook of Texas records that date.

The railroad had been built in the mid-1880s to connect Taylor with Bastrop and on toward Houston. When the tracks arrived, the small community of Smithville moved about two miles west to meet them. In **1894**, the Katy placed its central shops in Smithville, and the town grew quickly. It was incorporated the next year. The James H. Long Railroad Park and Museum on the Smithville depot grounds still tells that story.

## This month in history: Nov. 18, 1943 — the 102nd Infantry Division arrives at Camp Swift

The same November date, 52 years later, belongs to Camp Swift. A unit history of the Special Troops of the **102nd Infantry Division** says that on **Nov. 18, 1943**, after two months of field duty in the Louisiana maneuvers, the division moved to Camp Swift for post-maneuver training.

Camp Swift, seven miles north of Bastrop between U.S. 95 and U.S. 290, had been activated on **May 4, 1942**. The Handbook of Texas says it was built on 55,906 acres and at first had 2,750 buildings, designed to hold 44,000 troops. During World War II it trained infantry divisions and many other units, and it later held German prisoners of war. The reservation is still used today as a Texas Army National Guard training site.

## This month in history: Nov. 29, 1995 — Smithville's residential historic district is listed

On **Nov. 29, 1995**, the Smithville Residential Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places, reference number 95001395. The district is roughly bounded by Cleveland, First, Mills, North 9th, Burleson, and Colorado streets and the Colorado River. The National Park Service list for Bastrop County records 191 buildings in the district.

The listing came a century after Smithville's railroad boom and incorporation. It recognizes a neighborhood of houses and related buildings from the town's growth in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## This month in history: Nov. 30, 1890 — John Holland Jenkins dies in Bastrop

On **Nov. 30, 1890**, John Holland Jenkins died in Bastrop and was buried at Fairview Cemetery. The Handbook of Texas, citing contemporary accounts, says he had been injured on Nov. 11 in a gunfight that involved his sons and died 19 days later of "a brief illness of heart disease."

Jenkins was an early settler in what became Bastrop County. As a teenager he served in the San Jacinto campaign, though he was sent to help his family and neighbors reach safety during the Runaway Scrape and was not at the battle itself. He later served as a Texas Ranger and, in the 1880s, wrote a memoir that his great-great-grandson edited and published in 1958 as *Recollections of Early Texas*. He also collected and published other early settlers' memories in the *Bastrop Advertiser*.
