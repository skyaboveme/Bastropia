A new water-supply study for eastern Travis County names groundwater from Bastrop and Lee counties as one of its two best options for meeting future demand. That matters to residents here, three days before a groundwater rules hearing.

The [Eastern Crescent Water Study](https://www.kxan.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/40/2026/11/2026_Travis-County-Eastern-Crescent-Water-Supply-Report.pdf) covers the fast-growing area east of I-35 and Austin along the SH 130 corridor. It was presented to Travis County commissioners this week, according to [KVUE](https://www.kvue.com/article/tech/science/environment/study-warns-potential-billion-gallon-water-shortfall-eastern-travis-county-texas/269-e70b1d81-5982-4217-b597-5e692b37b695) and [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/local/travis-county/eastern-travis-county-could-face-nearly-5-billion-gallon-water-shortage-by-2080/). Using projections from the 2026 Region K Water Plan, it estimates that water utilities in that area could fall short by about 15,000 acre-feet a year by 2080. That's roughly 4.9 billion gallons. [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/local/travis-county/eastern-travis-county-could-face-nearly-5-billion-gallon-water-shortage-by-2080/) reported this is enough water for up to 50,000 Texas homes. The study, prepared by Freese and Nichols and Richards Consulting, says growth there has outpaced infrastructure investment. [KVUE](https://www.kvue.com/article/tech/science/environment/study-warns-potential-billion-gallon-water-shortfall-eastern-travis-county-texas/269-e70b1d81-5982-4217-b597-5e692b37b695) noted that the projections don't mean communities are running out of water today.

Of the 15 options the consultants reviewed, two scored highest. One is buying water from Recharge Water. According to the study as reported by [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/local/travis-county/eastern-travis-county-could-face-nearly-5-billion-gallon-water-shortage-by-2080/), Recharge Water controls rights to 46,000 acre-feet of groundwater a year in Lee and Bastrop counties, and about 28,000 acre-feet of that is not under contract. [The Cool Down](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-tech/eastern-crescent-texas-water-shortage-study/), citing the study, reported that Recharge Water said deliveries could begin within 24 months of a signed contract, with price still to be negotiated. The other top option is a regional system to pump and treat brackish (salty) groundwater from Travis and Williamson counties. The study calls that the only option that could meet the full 2080 need on its own.

**What it means here**

Pumping in Bastrop and Lee counties is regulated by the Lost Pines Groundwater Conservation District. The study doesn't decide anything, and no contract has been reported. But it shows how much interest there is in Bastrop County's aquifers from outside the county.

The district's board holds a public hearing on proposed rule changes **Wednesday, Nov. 18, at 3:30 p.m.** at its office, 317 E. Hempstead St. in Giddings. The draft rules include sections on fees, groundwater management zones and brackish groundwater permitting. Residents can speak in person or file written comments. Materials are posted in the Agenda Center at [lostpineswater.org](https://www.lostpineswater.org/AgendaCenter). The district's phone number is 512-360-5088.
