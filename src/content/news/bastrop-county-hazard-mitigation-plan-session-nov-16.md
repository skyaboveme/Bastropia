Bastrop County's Office of Emergency Management has scheduled a working session for Monday, Nov. 16, at 2 p.m. to review the county's Hazard Mitigation Plan and talk through possible future projects.

According to the [county's calendar listing](https://bastropia.com/events/bastrop-county-hazard-mitigation-plan-workshop-2026-11-16), the session is in person at the Mike Fisher Building, 1501 Business Park Drive, in Bastrop. The listing says a quorum of the Commissioners Court may attend, but the court will not take any action at the meeting.

**Why it matters**

A hazard mitigation plan is the county's roadmap for lowering the damage from disasters before they happen. Here, that means wildfire, flooding, severe storms, drought and extreme heat. These plans spell out which risks a community faces and list projects to reduce them, like drainage work, wildfire fuel reduction or backup power for critical sites. The Federal Emergency Management Agency generally requires local governments to keep a current, approved plan to qualify for certain mitigation grants. That's why the project list discussed in sessions like this one can shape what gets funded later.

Bastrop County residents know these hazards well, from the 2011 Bastrop County Complex Fire to repeated flooding along the Colorado River and local creeks. A working session is where county staff and partners look at what's in the current plan and what should be added.

**If you want to go**

- **When:** Monday, Nov. 16, 2 p.m. The calendar lists 2–3 p.m.
- **Where:** Mike Fisher Building, 1501 Business Park Drive, Bastrop
- **What:** A working session to discuss the current plan and propose future projects. No Commissioners Court action.

The county's listing doesn't say whether public comment will be taken. If you have a specific concern, like a road that floods or an area with heavy brush near homes, you can contact the Bastrop County Office of Emergency Management ahead of time.
