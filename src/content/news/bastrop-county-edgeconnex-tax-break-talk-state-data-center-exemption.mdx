Tax breaks for data centers are on the table at two levels of government this week: in the Bastrop County courtroom on Monday, and at the Texas Capitol as lawmakers prepare for the 2027 session.

**Monday at the county**

Bastrop County Commissioners Court meets **Monday, Nov. 23, at 9 a.m.** at 804 Pecan St. in Bastrop. Precinct 1 Commissioner Butch Carmack placed an item on the agenda to discuss EdgeConneX, "including potential tax abatements" and the community benefits the company is proposing. The item is listed for discussion only, so no vote is scheduled. The one-page backup memo posted with the agenda does not give dollar amounts or terms.

EdgeConneX is building three data center campuses along FM 535 in Cedar Creek. Community Impact reported in October that state construction filings for nine buildings total about 5.83 million square feet and roughly $5.84 billion in estimated construction costs. In October, KVUE reported that some residents said they had not known the county approved EdgeConneX's plans and gave the company tax incentives in 2024.

A county tax abatement is a local agreement. A county agrees to collect less property tax on new development for a set number of years, usually in exchange for investment, jobs or other commitments. It is separate from the state sales tax break described below.

**Meanwhile, at the Capitol**

Community Impact reported Friday that several lawmakers want to repeal the state sales tax exemption that lets qualifying data centers buy electricity, cooling equipment, software and other infrastructure without paying sales tax. The exemption passed in 2013. The state comptroller's office puts its cost at more than $1 billion a year, up from about $15 million a year when it took effect in 2014.

At least four House bills had been filed on the topic, and a Senate proposal from Sen. Joan Huffman, R-Houston, had the support of 23 senators and senators-elect, according to Community Impact. Supporters of repeal say the money could go to schools or property tax relief. Defenders, including a Rice University economist and the Texas Taxpayers and Research Association, argue data centers add to local tax bases and the state economy, and that repeal could push some projects elsewhere.

The legislative session begins **Jan. 12, 2027**.

**How to weigh in Monday**

Residents can speak on agenda items by filling out the county's Public Participation Form and giving it to the County Clerk before the court reaches the item. Speakers usually get three minutes. The court also meets on the 2026 certified tax levy and other items. Bastropia's full preview is at https://bastropia.com/news/bastrop-county-commissioners-court-nov-23-2026-agenda-preview.
