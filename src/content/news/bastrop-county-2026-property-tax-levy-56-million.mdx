Bastrop County's property tax bills for 2026 now have a total. The county's certified tax roll puts this year's county levy at about **$56.4 million**, and commissioners are asked to approve it at 9 a.m. today.

**What the roll shows**

Tax Assessor-Collector Ellen Owens sent the 2026 certified roll to Commissioners Court on Nov. 13. The levy is the total tax calculated on the certified appraisal roll at the rate the court already adopted.

The county levy is $56,419,737.80 at a rate of $0.3774 per $100 of taxable value. That splits into two pieces:

- **Operations:** $0.3107 per $100, or $46,378,690.37.
- **Debt payments:** $0.0667 per $100, or $10,041,047.43.

A second roll covers the county's Farm-to-Market Lateral Road and Bridge District 1, which pays for road and bridge upkeep. Its levy is $11,815,034.96 at $0.078 per $100.

The tax office says the figures are as of Nov. 13. Changes from later appraisal updates will be reported separately.

**How this fits the rate set in September**

Those two rates add up to the $0.4554 per $100 county rate commissioners adopted on Sept. 14. Last year's rate was $0.4287. The county's tax rate order estimated the increase would raise operations taxes on a $100,000 home by about $26.70. The adopted budget's cover page says it will bring in $6,241,810 more in property taxes than last year, a 9.56% increase.

**Why commissioners vote on it**

State law (Texas Property Tax Code Section 26.09(e)) requires the governing body to approve the tax roll. The court is also asked to approve 2026 rolls for the Double Eagle Ranch public improvement districts 1 and 2. The vote does not change the rate.

**What it means for your bill**

The county share is only part of most property tax bills. School districts, cities and other local taxing units set their own rates. Texas property taxes are generally due by the end of January, so check your bill for the exact date.

Questions about a bill go to the Bastrop County Tax Office at 211 Jackson St. in Bastrop, 512-332-7266.
