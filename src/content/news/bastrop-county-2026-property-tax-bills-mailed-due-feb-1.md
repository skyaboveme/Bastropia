Property owners across Bastrop County should start finding their 2026 tax bills in the mailbox this week.

Tax Assessor-Collector Ellen Owens told Community Impact her office began mailing about **75,660** property tax records the week of Nov. 23. You have until **Feb. 1, 2027**, to pay without penalties or interest.

**Why the bills came later this year**

Owens said her office had to wait for Bastrop ISD's Nov. 3 tax rate election, because the result decided the district's rate. Voters turned down Proposition A, which would have raised the district's maintenance and operations rate by 3 cents per $100 of value.

**What changed on your bill**

Each taxing unit sets its own rate, so the direction depends on where you live. According to Community Impact:

- Bastrop County's rate went up.
- Bastrop ISD's rate and the county road district rate went down.
- Bastrop County ESD No. 3, which took over operations Oct. 1, kept its rate the same.
- The city of Bastrop's rate rose slightly, even though the city adopted its no-new-revenue rate.

For scale, the county's certified 2026 levy, which went to commissioners on Nov. 23, is about **$56.4 million** at a rate of $0.3774 per $100 of value.

**New ways to pay**

The tax office has a new payment website at https://bastrop.propertytaxpayments.net/. Owens said owners can now:

- Pay several accounts in one shopping-cart checkout.
- Create a login to save accounts and preferences.
- Print payment receipts.

You can also pay in person at any of the county's **four** tax offices.

**Questions?**

Contact the tax office at taxoffice@co.bastrop.tx.us or 512-581-7161. If your bill looks wrong, call before the deadline rather than after.
