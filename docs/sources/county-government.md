## How the county is organized

Bastrop County is a Texas county. The Commissioners Court is the governing body: the County Judge (presiding officer, countywide) and four commissioners elected from precincts. The court adopts the budget and tax rate, oversees county roads, sets many fees and policies, and approves plats and related development items in the unincorporated area.

The county **does not have general zoning** in the unincorporated area (Development Services FAQ, https://www.bastropcounty.gov/page/ds.home, checked Nov. 16, 2026). It does administer floodplain rules, on-site sewage (OSSF) permits, development permits, subdivision regulations, and limited residential building-code requirements for certain single-family and duplex construction. There is also a small area of airport zoning related to Austin Executive Airport in the northwest part of the county, under the Airport Zoning Act.

Cities of Bastrop, Elgin, and Smithville are separate municipal governments. This file covers **county** government. School districts, ESDs, MUDs, the appraisal district, and the Lost Pines Groundwater Conservation District are separate entities that often intersect the county beat.

Main switchboard / courthouse: 804 Pecan Street, Bastrop, TX 78602. Phone listed in the site footer: (512) 581-7100. https://www.bastropcounty.gov/

### Public comment

Rules of Procedure, Conduct and Decorum (still the posted rules; adopted Dec. 10, 2012, under then-Judge Paul Pape): https://www.bastropcounty.gov/upload/page/0331/docs/scanned_rules_of_commissioners_court.pdf

Public Participation Form: https://www.bastropcounty.gov/upload/page/0331/docs/Public_Participation_Form.pdf  
Also linked from the clerk’s page.

Practical rules from those documents:

- Meetings (regular, special, emergency) are open; executive sessions are not.
- To speak, fill out the Public Participation Form and give it to the **County Clerk before** the court takes up the item.
- Speakers are generally limited to **three minutes**. Time is kept by the clerk or a designee.
- Public comment on any one agenda item is capped at **30 minutes**. If more than 10 people want to speak on that item, only 10 are recognized, split as evenly as possible for and against.
- The court may shorten or lengthen those limits by majority vote on a matter of “exceptional interest.”
- “Citizen comments” on subjects **not** on the agenda: the Texas Open Meetings Act bars the court and staff from deliberating on those inquiries during that item (the form itself cites Gov’t Code §551.042).
- Agenda placement for a regular meeting: a request must be filed and approved by a member of the court and/or the County Judge by **5:00 p.m. Tuesday** before the meeting.
- The County Judge presides and is a voting member. The sheriff or a deputy acts as bailiff. Press may not place equipment on or within three feet of the bench without the presiding judge’s permission; interviews are not to be conducted in the courtroom while the court is in session.

### Parks

## Major current issues

These themes show up repeatedly in 2026 agendas and local coverage. They are context, not finished stories.

**Growth and subdivision activity.** Regular agendas are heavy with preliminary plats, final plats, replats, lot-frontage variances (§VI.3.A of the subdivision regulations), accepting roads into county maintenance, and fiscal surety / maintenance bonds. A 2026 update of the subdivision regulations is posted: https://www.bastropcounty.gov/upload/page/0157/docs/Bastrop%20County%20Subdivision%20Regulations%20-%202026%20Update.pdf  
The county has no general zoning in the unincorporated area, so plat rules, floodplain, OSSF, and road standards are the main county levers.

**Data centers and the Pacifico CCK Energy project.** Resident concern about data-center development, especially around Cedar Creek, has been a live political issue (it also featured in the Precinct 4 race, per Smithville Texas News). On Oct. 13 the court considered asking TCEQ for a public meeting on Pacifico CCK Energy’s proposed electric generating unit. Local TV and other outlets have described a large proposed gas plant tied to data-center load. Follow TCEQ, the county resolution, and any city action separately, and do not collapse “proposed” into “approved.”

**County land-use authority.** The Sep. 28 agenda included a resolution regarding a bill under Local Government Code Chapter 231 to protect “sensitive areas and the rural areas” of the county — a legislative-ask story, not a new local ordinance by itself.

**Roads and bond / capital road work.** Debt service rose in the FY 2026–2027 rate ($0.06670 vs. $0.04371). The budget cover lists $127.1 million in remaining property-tax-secured principal. Individual road acceptances (for example Silverleaf in Precinct 1, Moon Mist/Ripple Brook, Lake Mist and Lake Leaf Drives) appear often. Do not describe a new voter “road bond” unless a specific bond election or issuance item is on an agenda.

**Hazard mitigation and disaster recovery.** OEM has been running hazard-mitigation plan working sessions (Nov. 9 and Nov. 16, 2026). A Nov. 13 working session covered “ongoing recovery projects.” The Nov. 16 special session includes a GLO Mitigation Reallocation Program application. Burn-ban extensions appeared on Sep. 14 and Sep. 28 agendas.

**Water.** Groundwater is primarily a Lost Pines GCD issue (Bastrop and Lee counties), not a Commissioners Court utility. The district’s agenda center is https://www.lostpineswater.org/AgendaCenter . A separate Eastern Crescent water-study story (Travis County / SH 130 corridor, affecting growth east of Austin) has been in the regional news; it is not a Bastrop County Court adoption. WCID #3 and many MUDs also set their own rates (see CAD taxing-unit list).

**Public health and EMS.** The Public Health Department is relatively new as a county department (priorities were set by a task force in 2024). Its FY 2025–2026 annual report was a Nov. 9 department report. ESD #3 (EMS) also updated the court that day. Bluebonnet Trails jail-based mental health services appeared on the Oct. 13 consent agenda.

**Facilities.** Development Services building construction and a Judicial Complex master-developer RFQ are the two large facility leads (see story leads). Courthouse roof-repair change orders were ratified on the Oct. 13 agenda. An ARPA radio-system Motorola change order was on Oct. 26.

**Elections administration and transition.** Official canvass Nov. 16; a campaign-finance software item and an elections electioneering policy were on recent agendas; compensatory time for elections staff was on Oct. 13. Election precinct boundaries were revised for 2026 (notice and maps on the GIS and elections sites). Incoming County Judge and two JP transitions are a January story if the unofficial results are canvassed as reported.
